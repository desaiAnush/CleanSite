# Leftover Job Waste: Options for Painters and Small Trade Businesses in Centre County (Summary for CleanSite)

## 1. Who this file is for
- A painter or small trade business with leftover paint, stain, thinner, rags, or spray cans from a job (not from their own home).
- Under PA DEP rules, household waste is exempt, but waste a business generates is regulated (kb01 section 1).
- Most small painting businesses will likely fall in the smallest category, Very Small Quantity Generator (VSQG): 220 lb (100 kg) or less of hazardous waste per calendar month (kb01 section 2). The business must still check this itself.

## 2. First options for any leftover job paint (no special rules needed)
- Use it up on the next job, or give usable paint away. CCRRA lists theater groups, church groups, shelters, and community organizations. Habitat for Humanity ReStore accepts latex paint; call (814) 353-2390 for specifications (kb02 section 2).
- Keep lids tight and label each can with color and purchase date so it stays usable (kb02 section 2).
- GAP: CCRRA's drying guidance (let small amounts dry, or mix with kitty litter or paint hardener, then trash) is written for households. No source confirms it applies to a business's job waste. Refer to CCRRA (814-238-7005).

## 3. CCRRA options and job waste
- **Spring HHW event:** The 2026 event was April 24 (8 a.m. to 4 p.m.) and April 25 (8 a.m. to 2 p.m.) at 253 Transfer Road, Bellefonte. Free, registration required. The 2026 newsletter says items are accepted "from households only." Latex paint is not accepted. (Source 5.)
- So job waste is very likely not accepted at the HHW event. CleanSite should say this is household-only per CCRRA's newsletter and tell contractors to call CCRRA to confirm.
- **Transfer Station latex service:** up to 5 gallons of latex paint per person per day, $25.00 minimum charge (Source 6). The page does not say whether businesses can use it. GAP: refer to CCRRA.
- **Transfer Station and liquid oil-based paint:** not accepted (Source 6).
- **CCRRA does not accept "hazardous waste"** or liquid waste, and paint cans only with lids removed and interiors clean (kb02 section 4).

## 4. VSQG basics: what a small business must do (federal fact sheet)
EPA lists three basic VSQG requirements (Source 1):
1. Identify all hazardous waste you generate.
2. Never store more than 2,200 lb (1,000 kg) of hazardous waste on site at any time.
3. Make sure the waste gets delivered to an approved off-site facility.

Approved destinations for VSQG waste include (Source 1):
- A state or federally regulated hazardous waste treatment, storage, or disposal facility (TSDF)
- A facility permitted, licensed, or registered by a state to manage municipal or industrial solid waste
- A facility that legitimately uses, reuses, or recycles the waste
- A universal waste handler or destination facility
- NOTE: This is the federal list. PA can be stricter. CleanSite should not tell a business a specific local facility is approved unless a source says so.

## 5. Moving job waste yourself vs. hiring a hauler
- A VSQG may haul its own hazardous waste in PA without a transporter license, but must mark containers "Hazardous Waste" and label the hazards (kb01 section 3).
- SQGs and LQGs must hold a PA transporter license or hire a licensed PA hazardous waste transporter (kb01 section 3).
- To find a licensed hauler: PA DEP publishes an interactive "List of licensed hazardous waste transporters" linked from its Hazardous Waste Transportation page (Source 3). CleanSite should link to this list, not recommend a specific company.
- Most transporters and disposal facilities require a manifest even from a VSQG (kb01 section 4).

## 6. Oil-based paint, stain, and varnish from jobs (universal waste)
- PA treats oil-based finishes as universal waste. Aerosol cans (including spray paint) are also universal waste (Source 2; kb01 section 5).
- PA DEP says universal waste can be transported to a permitted treatment, storage, or disposal facility without a manifest or a licensed transporter (Source 2).
- Large quantity universal waste handlers must keep oil-based finishes in their original or other proper labeled packaging, put any leaking or damaged containers in a closed, sound, compatible container, and must not open, blend, or filter them (25 Pa. Code 266b.31).
- GAP: Small quantity handler rules (labels, time limits, training) are in DEP's Universal Waste Compliance Guide (2510-BK-DEP2564) and Fact Sheet (2510-FS-DEP2723), which are not yet in this knowledge base. Refer to DEP or EMAP.
- GAP: No source names a facility in or near Centre County that accepts universal waste oil-based finishes from businesses.

## 7. Thinner-soaked rags from jobs
- PA follows the federal solvent-contaminated wipes exclusion: closed, non-leaking containers labeled "Excluded Solvent-Contaminated Wipes," stored on site no more than 180 days, no free liquids when sent out. Reusable wipes go to a regulated laundry; disposable wipes go to a regulated landfill or combustion facility. Wipes with trichloroethylene are not covered (kb01 section 6).
- CleanSite should also give the fire-safety point already in kb02: CCRRA warns of spontaneous combustion risk with paint waste in the trash.
- GAP: No Centre County source names where a business can send excluded wipes.

## 8. Free help for small businesses
- **EMAP (Environmental Management Assistance Program):** free and confidential environmental help for small businesses, run in partnership with PA DEP. Phone 877-ASK-EMAP (877-275-3627). Website askemap.org (Source 4).
- **PA DEP Small Business Assistance Program:** 717-787-9702, ra-epsbcac@pa.gov. DEP's Small Business Ombudsman serves businesses with 100 or fewer full-time employees (Source 4).
- **PA DEP Hazardous Waste Division:** 717-787-6239, RA-HazWaste@pa.gov (kb01 section 9).
- **CCRRA:** 814-238-7005 (kb02).
- CleanSite should point contractors to EMAP when the answer depends on their specific business situation. It is free and confidential, which may feel less risky than calling a regulator.

## 9. Household vs. job: what changes in the answer
| Item | From your house | From a job |
|---|---|---|
| Latex paint | Dry it and trash it, or Transfer Station (5 gal/day, $25 min) | Use up/donate first; Transfer Station eligibility not confirmed, call CCRRA |
| Oil-based paint/stain | Spring HHW event (register) or dry it | HHW event is households only; treat as universal waste; hauler list or EMAP |
| Spray paint cans | Spring HHW event | Universal waste; hauler list or EMAP |
| Thinner rags | HHW event lists flammables | Solvent wipes exclusion rules (labeled closed container, 180 days) |
| Paint thinner (liquid) | Spring HHW event | Likely hazardous waste; count toward monthly total; hauler list or EMAP |
